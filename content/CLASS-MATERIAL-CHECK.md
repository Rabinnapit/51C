### The changes that matter most for the exam

- **Vision of the FAR.** Class: 1.102(a): deliver best value on a timely basis, maintain public trust, fulfill public policy objectives. Now: Replaced by seven guiding principles: mission first, effective use of taxpayer dollars, maximize commercial, award to contractors with a superior ability to perform, competition, integrity, and decisions at the lowest level.
- **Common forms.** Class: SF 33 = sealed bid; DD 1409 = abstract of offers. Now: The SF 33 (Solicitation, Offer and Award) is used for sealed bids and RFPs. The abstract of offers is the SF 1409 (FAR 14.303(g)). The others on the list are right.
- **Notice exemptions.** Class: FAR 5.202 exceptions; urgency lets you shorten the synopsis time (5.203). Now: Exemptions are in 5.101(b). Other-than-full-and-open authorities are exempt except only-one-source and national security; urgency needs no notice, and its justification is posted within 30 days after award.
- **Simplified acquisition procedures.** Class: All SAP is in Part 13; commercial SAP up to $7.5 million; oral solicitations allowed under 13.106-1. Now: Part 13 is now noncommercial only, at or below the SAT. Commercial simplified procedures: 12.201-1, up to $9 million ($15 million in special cases). Micro-purchases: subpart 12.4. The RFO dropped the oral-quotation text.
- **Commercial provisions and clauses.** Class: Three standard provisions (52.212-1, -2, -3) and two clauses (52.212-4, -5) under FAR 12.301. Now: 52.212-3 and 52.212-5 are reserved under the RFO. 12.205 requires 52.212-1 and 52.212-4, adds 52.212-2 when using other procedures, and lists other provisions and clauses in Tables 12-2 and 12-3.
- **Part 15 exchanges.** Class: Three exchanges: clarifications, communications (before the competitive range), discussions. Now: Two: clarifications (any time after receipt to award, no revisions) and negotiations with offerors in the competitive range. Communications is gone as a separate step.
- **Certified cost or pricing data.** Class: Required over $2 million. Now: $2.5 million under the FAR; $10 million for DoW contracts awarded after 30 June 2026. Never at or below the SAT.
- **Nonconformance classes.** Class: Minor = likely to materially reduce usability; major = likely to result in failure. Now: Wrong on the slide. Major = likely to fail OR materially reduce usability; minor = NOT likely to materially reduce usability; critical = hazardous or unsafe.

## Test-taker topics

- **Know the 20 FAR part titles cold.** Several questions just ask which part covers a topic. Use the RFO titles: Part 13 is Simplified Procedures for Noncommercial Acquisitions; Part 19 is Small Business. Lesson: The rulebook: FAR, DFARS and AFARS.
- **Bilateral versus unilateral modifications.** One test taker counted about 10 questions. If the contractor must agree, bilateral; if a clause gives the Government the right (changes, options, admin, funding, termination notice), unilateral (43.203). Lesson: Changes and modifications.
- **UCF sections, especially B, D, H, I and M.** B = supplies or services and prices; D = packaging and marking; H = special requirements; I = clauses; M = evaluation factors. K, L and M are not physically in the contract; K is incorporated by reference. Lesson: Publicizing and the solicitation.
- **PIID position 9.** The ninth character is the instrument type: A BPA, B IFB, C contract, D indefinite-delivery, F order, G BOA, P purchase order, Q RFQ, R RFP (4.201, Table 4-1). Lesson: Write it down: the contract file.
- **Tradeoff versus LPTA.** Paying $5.5 million instead of $4 million means a tradeoff. The RFO adds a third approach: highest technically rated with a fair and reasonable price (15.103). Lesson: Source selection: choosing the winner.
- **Exchanges after proposals arrive.** Older notes say three methods (clarifications, communications, discussions). Under the RFO: clarifications and negotiations only (15.202, 15.204-2). Lesson: Source selection: choosing the winner.
- **Late bids and late proposals.** Late means after the exact time. Only listed exceptions apply, and only if accepting does not unduly delay the buy; a late improvement to the winning offer can always be accepted (14.301(f), 15.107(b)). Lesson: Source selection: choosing the winner.
- **Certified cost or pricing data: when and when not.** Never at or below the SAT. Required at $2.5 million (FAR) or above $10 million (DoW, awards after 30 June 2026) unless adequate price competition, prices set by law, commercial, or a waiver applies. Lesson: Cost analysis, certified data and cost principles.
- **Adequate price competition.** Two or more responsible offerors competing independently. For DoW, one offer is not enough; resolicit for 30 days when the solicitation allowed fewer than 30 (DFARS 215.371-2). Lesson: Cost analysis, certified data and cost principles.
- **Cost analysis versus price analysis.** Price analysis checks the total; cost analysis checks the elements and is required with certified data. Cost realism is required for cost-reimbursement contracts (15.404). Lesson: Price analysis: is the price fair and reasonable?.
- **Protest venues, order and timing.** Agency, GAO, Court of Federal Claims; no required order. Solicitation defects before closing; others 10 days at GAO; agency goal 35 days; GAO 100 days (33.104, 33.105). Lesson: Protests.
- **Protest versus claim.** A protest objects to a solicitation or award; a claim is a demand by a party to an existing contract (33.102, 2.101). Lesson: Disputes and claims.
- **Your first action on a protest.** Consult legal counsel (33.103(a)); for agency protests notify the HCA; withhold award or suspend performance as the rules require. Lesson: Protests.
- **Claims and REAs: when to certify.** Claims over $100,000 must be certified (2.101). DoW also requires certification of requests for equitable adjustment over the SAT (DFARS 252.243-7002). Lesson: Disputes and claims.
- **ADR is voluntary.** By definition (33.201). Policy is to resolve disputes at the contracting officer's level by agreement (33.204). Lesson: Disputes and claims.
- **Three terminations.** Convenience (no longer needed), default (noncommercial, contractor fault, Part 49), cause (commercial, contractor fault, 52.212-4(m)). Cure notice with 10 or more days left; show cause with fewer. Lesson: Terminations.
- **Government property.** Contractors furnish their own property ordinarily (45.102). Liability for loss depends on contract type (45.104). Lesson: Running the contract after award.
- **Inspection, acceptance and risk of loss.** Acceptance is conclusive except latent defects, fraud and gross mistakes. Risk passes at acceptance or delivery at destination, whichever is later (46.505). Lesson: Quality: inspection, acceptance and warranties.
- **Postaward orientation versus debriefing.** Orientation: with the winner, about performance. Debriefing: with offerors, about the evaluation; request in 3 days, held within 5 (15.301-1). Lesson: Award, notices and debriefings.
- **When the source selection decision document is written.** After the comparative assessment and before award. It records the SSA's rationale and tradeoffs (15.205). Lesson: Source selection: choosing the winner.
- **Closeout timing.** FFP 6 months after physical completion; indirect rates 36 months; all others 20 months; simplified buys on receipt and final payment (Table 4-2). Lesson: Closeout.
- **Contract file retention.** Contracts and related records: 6 years after final payment (4.309, Table 4-3). Lesson: Write it down: the contract file.
- **Prompt payment.** 30 days after a proper invoice or acceptance, whichever is later. 15 days for small business is a goal (32.009). Lesson: Money rules: Purpose, Time and Amount.
- **Advance payments.** Least preferred financing. Look at private and customary financing first (32.106). Lesson: Running the contract after award.
- **Options in thresholds.** Thresholds use the final anticipated value including all options (1.107(c)). Lesson: The rulebook: FAR, DFARS and AFARS.
- **Delegation and warrants.** Every authority is delegable unless the FAR says otherwise (1.107(b)). A warrant (SF 1402) states your limits (1.403-2). Lesson: Who can bind the Government.
- **Organizational conflicts of interest and gifts.** OCI: impaired objectivity, biased advice or unfair advantage (2.101, subpart 9.5). Never solicit gifts from contractors (3.101-2). Lesson: Ethics and integrity.
- **Describing agency needs.** SOW (how), PWS (outcomes and standards), SOO (objectives; offeror writes the PWS). Requirements must not unduly restrict competition (Part 11). Lesson: Describing the need: SOW, PWS and SOO.
- **Market research timing.** Required before new requirements, before solicitations over the SAT, and before orders over the SAT (10.001(b)). Lesson: Market research.
- **Systems: EDA and CBAR.** EDA (in PIEE) is where DoW distributes contract documents. CBAR (DCMA) holds contractor business data and negotiation records for pricing. Lesson: The systems you will use.
- **Exam format.** Test takers describe about 80% scenarios and 20% recall. Understand why a rule exists so you can apply it to a new story. Lesson: How to pass CON 3990V.
